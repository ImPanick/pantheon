## When to Use

Asked to summarise, recap, give a TL;DR, pull out key points or action items, or explain what a long piece of content says.

## Procedure

1. Know what the reader needs: a decision, the gist, action items, or what changed. Default to the gist plus anything they must act on.
2. Read the whole thing before writing; skim-and-summarise misses the turn at the end.
3. Open with the one or two sentences that matter most — the conclusion, decision or answer.
4. Then the essentials, in order of importance: decisions made, numbers and dates exactly as written, who owns what, open questions and risks.
5. Keep attribution: who said or claims what. Never merge two people's views into one.
6. Match length to the source and the need — a few lines for a short thread, a short structured summary for a long report. Bullets for lists of items, prose for an argument.
7. Say what you left out if it might matter ("the appendix's raw figures are not covered").

## Pitfalls

- Restating the introduction instead of the conclusion.
- Rounding or paraphrasing numbers and dates.
- Adding opinions or facts that are not in the source.

## Verification

- Someone who reads only the summary would make the same decision as someone who read the source.
